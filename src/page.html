How the score works

The score is an early score: it uses 5 of the 21 measures we plan. The other 16 show "not enough data" until we find an open, official source for them.

What is counted

  • Area: a 2 km circle around each locality's centre point. This is not an official ward boundary. The new 300-ward map has no published boundaries yet, so every locality gets the same size circle. We will switch once official boundaries are out.
  • Schools, healthcare, daily needs: places from Overture Maps (release of 18 Nov 2026) inside the circle. Daily needs means groceries, supermarkets, pharmacies, banks and ATMs. Places marked closed, or listed with confidence below 0.5, are left out. Counts show how many places there are, not how good they are.
  • Metro access: straight-line distance to the nearest operating metro station. Stations still under construction are left out.
  • Green cover: share of land in the circle under trees, shrubs, grass or wetland, from ESA WorldCover 2021 satellite land cover at 10 m.

From numbers to 0 to 100

  • Counts use a log scale against the locality with the most, so one very large count doesn't flatten the rest. The best locality gets 100.
  • Metro access gets 100 within 500 m of a station, falling in a straight line to 0 at 5 km or more.
  • Green cover is scaled against the greenest locality, which gets 100.
  • The score is the average of the five. Every measure weighs the same, and a measure you pick under "What matters to you" counts three times. These weights are provisional, awaiting the owner's approval.

Scores are relative: each locality is compared with the others on this site, so adding a locality can change everyone's numbers. Metro distance moves the ranking a lot. Localities far from an operating station, such as Kokapet and Financial District, score low mainly for that reason. Planned lines aren't counted because Metro Phase 2 isn't approved yet.
